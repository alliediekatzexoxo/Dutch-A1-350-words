## 1 · Sounds (klanken)

Dutch spelling is quite regular. Learn these sounds first and you can read almost anything aloud.

| Spelling | Sounds like (English) | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aa | long "ah" | naam name |
| oe | "oo" as in food | boek book |
| ie | "ee" as in see | bier beer |
| ij / ei | between "ay" and "eye" | tijd time, ei egg |
| ui | "ow" with rounded lips | huis house |
| eu | like French "peu" | deur door |
| au / ou | "ow" as in cow | auto car, oud old |
| g / ch | rough throat sound, like Scottish "loch" | goed good, acht eight |
| j | "y" as in yes | ja yes |
| sch | "s" + throat "ch" | school school |

**Tip!** Every vowel is pure and short or long, never a glide. Double vowels (aa, ee, oo, uu) mean the vowel is *long*. That is why maken becomes maak in ik maak: the long vowel stays long.

### Sentences

Ik heet Sara.My name is Sara.

**heten** = to be called. Stem **heet** (**heten** minus -en). With **ik** you use the bare stem: no ending.

Hoe gaat het met je?How are you?

Question word first (**hoe**), verb second (**gaat**), then the subject. **je** = informal "you"; use **u** for strangers and older people: **Hoe gaat het met u?**

Goed, dank je wel. En met jou?Good, thank you. And you?

No verb needed: a short answer is fine. **jou** is the stressed form of **je**, used after **met**.

Ik kom uit Spanje.I come from Spain.

**uit** = out of / from (a country or city). **komen** → **ik kom**. Subject, then verb, then the rest: the basic word order.

Ik begrijp het niet.I don't understand.

**niet** = not, placed near the end. **het** here means "it". Handy phrases: **Kunt u dat herhalen?** (Can you repeat that?)

**Tip!** Alsjeblieft works for both "please" and "here you are". The polite form is alstublieft.

### The three golden verbs

|  | zijn (to be) | hebben (to have) | wonen (to live) · regular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ik | ben | heb | woon |
| jij | bent | hebt | woont |
| u | bent | hebt / heeft | woont |
| hij / zij / het | is | heeft | woont |
| wij / jullie / zij | zijn | hebben | wonen |

**The regular-verb recipe:** infinitive minus **-en** = stem. ik + stem · jij/u/hij/zij/het + stem + **t** · wij/jullie/zij = infinitive. When jij comes *after* the verb, drop the **t**: Woon jij in Utrecht?

### Sentences

Ik ben student.I am a student.

Unlike English, Dutch skips **een** for jobs and roles after **zijn**: **Ik ben student**, not "Ik ben een student".

Zij heeft een fiets.She has a bike.

**hebben** is irregular: hij/zij/het **heeft**. **een** = a / an for both **de**- and **het**-words.

Wij wonen in Amsterdam.We live in Amsterdam.

Regular verb: wij takes the full infinitive **wonen**. **in** = in (a place).

Woon jij in Utrecht?Do you live in Utrecht?

A yes/no question starts with the verb. Because **jij** follows the verb, the **-t** disappears: **woon jij**, not "woont jij".

Hij werkt bij een bank.He works at a bank.

Stem **werk** + **t** for hij. **bij** = at / near (a company, a person).

Zij zijn mijn vrienden.They are my friends.

**zij** can mean "she" or "they": look at the verb! **zij is** = she is, **zij zijn** = they are.

Ik maak het eten.I make the food.

Spelling rule: **maken** → stem **maak**. One long vowel in the stem is written double (**aa**) to stay long. Same for **praten → praat**, **eten → eet**, **slapen → slaap**.

**Numbers 21–99 go backwards!** Units first, then **en**, then tens, written as one word: 24 = vierentwintig (four-and-twenty), 57 = zevenenvijftig, 99 = negenennegentig. After a vowel with a diaeresis: tweeëntwintig (22), drieëndertig (33).

### Sentences

Ik ben twintig jaar oud.I am twenty years old.

**jaar** stays singular after a number. Asking age: **Hoe oud ben je?**

Het kost vijf euro.It costs five euros.

**kosten** → stem **kost**; hij/zij/het adds **t**, but the stem already ends in **t**, so only one is written. **euro** also stays singular.

Wij hebben drie kinderen.We have three children.

A number goes before the plural noun: **drie kinderen**. **kind → kinderen** is an irregular plural.

| English | Dutch | English | Dutch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Sentences

Dit is mijn zus.This is my sister.

**dit** = this. Possessives like **mijn** go before the noun and never change.

Zij heeft twee broers.She has two brothers.

Plural: **broer → broers** (add -s). Most plurals use **-en** or **-s** (see chapter 12).

Mijn vader werkt in Rotterdam.My father works in Rotterdam.

The subject can be more than one word: **mijn vader** still takes the "hij" form **werkt**.

Ons huis is klein, onze tuin is groot.Our house is small, our garden is big.

**ons** goes with **het**-words (het huis), **onze** with **de**-words (de tuin).

### Sentences

Ik wil een kop koffie.I want a cup of coffee.

**willen** is irregular: **ik wil**, **jij wilt/wil je**, **hij wil** (no -t). **een kop** = a cup.

Ik drink graag thee.I like drinking tea.

**graag** after the verb gives "gladly / like to". Dutch has no "like + -ing"; use **graag** instead.

Mag ik een broodje kaas, alsjeblieft?May I have a cheese sandwich, please?

**mogen** (may): **ik mag**. Starting with the verb makes it a polite request. No word for "of": **broodje kaas** = sandwich (of) cheese.

Wij eten rijst met kip.We eat rice with chicken.

**met** = with. **eten** (to eat) and **eten** (food) look the same. Context tells you which.

Ik heb honger en mijn zus heeft dorst.I am hungry and my sister is thirsty.

Dutch "has" hunger and thirst: **honger hebben**, **dorst hebben**, not "to be".

De rekening, alstublieft.The bill, please.

Restaurant classic. **alstublieft** is the polite form. Add **Kan ik pinnen?** (Can I pay by card?)

### Telling the time

| Clock | Dutch | Idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3:00 | drie uur | three o'clock |
| 3:15 | kwart over drie | quarter past three |
| 3:30 | half vier | half (way to) four |
| 3:45 | kwart voor vier | quarter to four |
| 3:10 | tien over drie | ten past three |

**Watch out!** half vier is 3:30, *not* 4:30. Dutch counts the half hour towards the *next* hour.

### Sentences

Is het vandaag dinsdag?Is it Tuesday today?

Yes/no question: verb first. The answer: **Ja, vandaag is het dinsdag.**

Morgen werk ik niet.Tomorrow I don't work.

**Verb always in second place!** When a time word starts the sentence, the subject moves behind the verb. Not "Morgen ik werk". This is called inversion.

Hoe laat is het?What time is it?

**hoe laat** = how late = what time. Answer: **Het is half drie.** (2:30)

Ik sta om zeven uur op.I get up at seven o'clock.

**opstaan** is a *separable verb*: **op** jumps to the end of the sentence. **om** = at (a clock time).

Mijn verjaardag is in mei.My birthday is in May.

**in** + month. **op** + day: **op maandag**. **om** + hour: **om acht uur**.

| de rode appel | het rode huis | een rode appel | een rood huis |

De appel is rood.The apple is red.

Adjective after **zijn**: no ending. **rood**, not "rode".

Het is een mooi huis.It is a beautiful house.

De soep is te warm.The soup is too hot.

**te** = too. **heel** = very: **De soep is heel lekker.**

bijat, near

### Sentences

Ik woon in Amsterdam.I live in Amsterdam.

**in** = where something is (location).

Ik ga met de fiets naar school.I go to school by bike.

**gaan**: ik ga, jij gaat, hij gaat, wij gaan. **naar** = to (direction). **met de fiets** = by bike. No article before **school** here.

Waar is het station?Where is the station?

**waar** = where, then verb, then subject.

Het station is naast de bank.The station is next to the bank.

Preposition + **de/het** + noun. **naast** never changes.

Ga rechtdoor en dan naar links.Go straight on and then left.

Commands use the bare stem: **ga** (from gaan), **kijk** (look), **wacht** (wait).

Ik ga naar huis.I am going home.

**naar huis** = (to) home. Fixed phrase, no article. **thuis** = at home: **Ik ben thuis.**

**Two verbs = verb 1 in 2nd place, verb 2 at the very end.** With willen, kunnen, moeten, mogen the second verb is an infinitive.

### Sentences

Ik kan goed zwemmen.I can swim well.

**kunnen** is irregular (ik kan). The second verb **zwemmen** goes to the end. **goed** = well.

Wij moeten nu gaan.We have to go now.

**moeten** + infinitive at the end. Dutch has no word for "to" here: just **moeten gaan**.

Zij spreekt Nederlands en Engels.She speaks Dutch and English.

**spreken** → stem **spreek** + t. Languages are capitalised in Dutch (**Nederlands, Engels**).

Ik leer Nederlands op school.I am learning Dutch at school.

Dutch has no "-ing" present: **ik leer** means both "I learn" and "I am learning".

Ik hou van muziek.I love music.

**houden van** + noun. Spelling: **houden → hou**. For people: **Ik hou van jou.**

Ik bel mijn moeder vanavond.I call my mother this evening.

Present tense also covers the near future when a time word is present: **vanavond** (this evening).

### Sentences

Wat is dit?What is this?

Question word + verb + subject.

Waarom leer je Nederlands?Why are you learning Dutch?

Answer with **omdat** (because): **Omdat ik in Nederland woon.** After **omdat** the verb goes to the *end*.

Heb je een broer?Do you have a brother?

Yes/no question: verb first, no "do" needed. Dutch has no helper verb like English "do".

Hoeveel kost het?How much does it cost?

Prices: **Het kost vijftien euro.**

Wanneer komt de bus?When does the bus come?

**komen** → **komt** for de bus (= it).

### Negation: niet vs geen

| Use | When | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| geen | negates **een** or nouns with no article | Ik heb geen broer. I have no brother. |
| niet | negates verbs, adjectives, "the" nouns | Ik werk niet. I don't work. |
| niet | before an adjective | Ik ben niet moe. I'm not tired. |

Ik heb geen tijd.I have no time.

**tijd** has no article here, so you negate it with **geen**. Never "niet een" or "niet tijd".

Wij eten het brood niet.We don't eat the bread.

Definite object (**het brood**): use **niet**, placed after the object at the end.

## 12 · de / het, plurals & little words (woorden)

### de or het?

**de** goes with about 75% of nouns (masculine and feminine); **het** goes with neuter nouns. Always learn the article *with* the word. Quick hints:

| Always het | Example |
| --- | --- |
| all diminutives (-je) | het huisje little house |
| most words for languages | het Nederlands Dutch |
| metals, colours as nouns | het goud gold |
| infinitive as noun | het eten the eating, food |

**The plural always uses de**, even for het-words: het boek → de boeken.

### Plurals

| Rule | Singular | Plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| most nouns +en | boek | boeken | book(s) |
| double vowel loses one | straat | straten | street(s) |
| s → z | huis | huizen | house(s) |
| f → v | brief | brieven | letter(s) |
| -er, -el, -en +s | tafel | tafels | table(s) |
| final vowel +'s | auto | auto's | car(s) |
| irregular | kind | kinderen | child, children |
| irregular | stad | steden | city, cities |

### Diminutives

Add **-je** for small or cute things. They become **het**-words: de kat → het katje, het kind → het kindje, de man → het mannetje, het brood → het broodje.

### Sentences

Ik heb veel boeken.I have many books.

**veel** = many/much; the plural noun **boeken** follows it.

De huizen zijn groot.The houses are big.

**huis → huizen** (s → z). The verb is plural: **zijn**.

Zij heeft een klein katje.She has a little kitten.

**het katje** is a het-word, so after **een** the adjective has no -e: **een klein katje**.

Ik heb twee auto's.I have two cars.

Words ending in a vowel take **'s** with the apostrophe.
